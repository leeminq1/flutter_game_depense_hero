# Stage Art Bible

## Purpose

Define the visual progression for the 30-stage campaign before more towers, prop sets, and background structures are produced.

This document exists so new art is generated against a stable world plan rather than one-off taste decisions.

## Campaign Visual Arc

The campaign should feel like one road into corruption, not six unrelated biomes.

Visual progression:

- Stages 1-5: frontier road and village outskirts
- Stages 6-10: bandit crossroads and watch roads
- Stages 11-15: grave fields and broken cemetery lanes
- Stages 16-20: cursed chapel belt and plague woods
- Stages 21-25: bastion approach roads and military ruins
- Stages 26-30: throne march, fortress walls, and infernal keep edge

Each bracket should keep one dominant ground language, one landmark family, and one accent material.

## Stage Brackets

### Stages 1-5: Frontier Road

Fantasy read:

- rural trade road
- wooden fencing
- carts, sacks, road signs, brush, pines

Main materials:

- warm dirt
- pale stone
- brown wood
- muted green cloth

Enemy fit:

- raiders
- scouts
- early ambushers

Tower feel:

- improvised, wood-heavy, village militia energy

### Stages 6-10: Bandit Crossroads

Fantasy read:

- defended roadside choke points
- stolen barricades
- rough camps
- abandoned outposts

Main materials:

- dusty road
- dark timber
- patched banners
- iron scraps

Enemy fit:

- raiders
- shield infantry
- early cult infiltrators

Tower feel:

- reinforced militia structures and hardier military shapes

### Stages 11-15: Grave Fields

Fantasy read:

- cemetery roads
- mausoleum remains
- crooked dead trees
- grave markers and bone piles

Main materials:

- gray stone
- moss green
- dead grass
- pale bone

Enemy fit:

- skeletons
- grave guards
- undead pressure

Tower feel:

- shrines, wards, magic posts, sacred or anti-undead silhouette support

### Stages 16-20: Cursed Chapel Belt

Fantasy read:

- fallen chapel district
- shattered stained stone
- occult altars
- blackened roots

Main materials:

- charcoal stone
- violet cloth
- bronze ritual metal
- candle fire

Enemy fit:

- cult adepts
- corrupted knights
- mixed undead and cult pressure

Tower feel:

- heavier magic and shrine language

### Stages 21-25: Bastion Approach

Fantasy read:

- ruined fort roads
- defensive rubble
- broken siege hardware
- stern military remnants

Main materials:

- iron
- dark red banners
- weathered oak
- cold gray wall stone

Enemy fit:

- elite armored factions
- late corrupted support units

Tower feel:

- disciplined siege-defense silhouettes

### Stages 26-30: Throne March

Fantasy read:

- cursed fortress edge
- braziers
- banners
- chainworks
- black road into gold-lit tyranny

Main materials:

- black stone
- brass and gold
- crimson cloth
- ember orange

Enemy fit:

- warlocks
- champions
- Bastion Overlord

Tower feel:

- strongest shape language and highest contrast

## Visual Rules Per Stage

Every stage should define:

- one ground palette family
- one path edge treatment
- one cheap ground motif family
- one landmark prop family
- one secondary clutter family
- one banner, crystal, fire, or ritual accent

Avoid mixing two strong landmark families in the same stage unless it is a boss or crest stage.

## Ground Motif Language By Bracket

Ground motifs should support the lane, not compete with it.

Anchor emphasis rule:

- spawn area should read slightly more worn and directional than the middle of the lane
- core approach should be cleaner, more legible, and slightly more intentional than the rest of the map
- large bends should get stronger texture on the outer edge, not the apex
- large props and landmarks should own their silhouette with quieter terrain directly around them

### Frontier Road

- packed dirt bands
- wagon-rut ovals
- tiny pebble dots
- light grass-edge tufts

### Bandit Crossroads

- trampled mud patches
- fork scuffs
- plank chips
- harsher road-edge wear

### Grave Fields

- cold soil mottling
- shallow sink patches
- dead-weed specks
- flat grave-stone chips

### Cursed Chapel Belt

- cracked slab seams
- soot smears
- tiny wax or stain pools
- restrained rune residue at the edges only

### Bastion Approach

- worn block seams
- rubble fans
- metal patch plates
- directional military scuffs

### Throne March

- obsidian plate seams
- ember dust
- narrow corruption veins
- soft scorch borders

## Crest Stage Rule

Stages `5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30` should introduce a stronger landmark or prop set.

Examples:

- Stage 5: large road gate or militia stockade
- Stage 10: bandit barricade fort
- Stage 15: mausoleum gate
- Stage 20: cursed chapel ruins
- Stage 25: broken bastion wall segment
- Stage 30: infernal gate, braziers, throne-road motifs

Current production note:

- these six crest stages now have dedicated stage-level decoration layouts in campaign data
- future environment work should preserve their authored identity instead of collapsing them back into bracket-generic scenes
- crest-stage preview sheets can now be generated to compare composition consistency before runtime tuning

## Asset Priority Rule

When generating art after this document:

1. Finish stage bracket landmark props
2. Finish shared clutter props
3. Finish tower second-pass detail
4. Finish upgrade variants
5. Finish special boss-stage landmark props

This keeps the battlefield tone coherent before polishing tower variants.
